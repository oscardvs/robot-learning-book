This is a written version of ETH Zürich's Robot Learning course — eleven lectures on
how robots learn to act, from the classical control that came before machine learning
to the generalist policies people are building now.

It exists because the recordings are good and long. Ten and a half hours of video is a
fine way to learn something the first time and a terrible way to look it up. So the
lectures were transcribed, the slides recovered, and the whole thing rewritten as prose
you can read at your own pace, search, and come back to.

## What is in each chapter

A chapter covers one lecture, but it is not a transcript. It is written to be read on
its own, in order, and it explains the reasoning rather than listing the points.

**Figures are the real slides.** Each one carries a link to the second of the recording
where it was on screen, so you can jump straight to the lecturer explaining it if the
caption is not enough.

**Equations name every symbol.** No equation appears without a plain-language reading of
what it says and why it has that shape.

**Editor's notes are marked.** Where the lecture slipped — a wrong date, an institution
misremembered — the correction sits in an amber note, kept visibly separate so you
always know which voice you are hearing.

**Some things run.** Value iteration, and the argument for why a policy outputs a
distribution, are live on the page rather than drawn. Push on them; they re-solve.

## What is finished

Not all of it. The chapter list in the sidebar is the real state of the work, and the
front page shows the count. Lectures without a chapter yet still have their slides and
their recording linked, so there is something to work from for all eleven.

## Where it came from

The course is [263-5911-00L, *Robot Learning: From Fundamentals to Foundation
Models*](https://cvg.ethz.ch/lectures/Robot-Learning/), taught by Oier Mees at ETH
Zürich in spring 2026, with Marc Pollefeys as course mentor. The lectures and the slide
content are theirs.

This site is not theirs, and it is not endorsed by them. It is a study companion written
from the public recordings. Where it is wrong, that is this book's fault and not the
course's — the recordings are linked on every chapter so you can always check.
