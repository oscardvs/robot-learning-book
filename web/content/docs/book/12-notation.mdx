\markboth\{Notation}\{Notation}

The lectures this book is compiled from are not internally consistent about symbols. That is normal in a lecture series, where each session stands alone, and unworkable in a book, where Chapter 5's critic has to be the same object as Chapter 4's. This appendix records the convention used throughout, the places where a symbol is deliberately reused, and — for readers checking the book against the recordings — every point at which the book writes a different symbol from the one on screen.

Two rules govern everything below. **The slides are the authority on content; this table is the authority on symbols.** And **a symbol means one thing per chapter**, with the handful of surviving exceptions listed in §Deliberate reuse.

## The two collisions worth knowing about

### Actor and critic

The lectures use <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} /> and <InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} /> in opposite senses in different places: the deep Q-network's parameters are <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} />, DDPG's actor is <InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} /> and its critic <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} />, the policy-gradient lecture's policy is <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} /> and its critic <InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} />, and the generative-model lecture's decoder is <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} /> and its encoder <InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} />.

This book resolves it in the direction that four of those five already agree with:

<Aside>
  **<InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} />** parameterizes the model that produces what gets deployed — the policy, the actor, the decoder, the noise predictor, the velocity field.
  **<InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} />** parameterizes the model that scores or infers — the critic (<InlineMath tex={"Q_\\phi"} />, <InlineMath tex={"V_\\phi"} />), the encoder or inference network (<InlineMath tex={"q_\\phi"} />).
</Aside>

An **overbar** always marks a frozen or slowly-updated copy: <InlineMath tex={"\\bar\\phi"} /> is a target critic, <InlineMath tex={"\\bar\\alpha_k"} /> a cumulative product held fixed by the schedule. Only Chapter 4's DDPG had to be flipped to comply, and the split has a reading that is easier to remember than a rule: _deployed model <InlineMath tex={"\\theta"} />, helper <InlineMath tex={"\\phi"} />._

### The diffusion step index

The lectures index the denoising process with <InlineMath tex={"i"} />, with <InlineMath tex={"k"} />, and with <InlineMath tex={"t"} /> in different places, and use <InlineMath tex={"t"} /> for continuous flow time. But <InlineMath tex={"t"} /> is the environment timestep everywhere else in the book, and <InlineMath tex={"i"} /> is a generic enumeration index.

<Aside>
  **<InlineMath tex={"k \\in \\{0,1,\\dots,K\\}"} />** is the discrete denoising step. **<InlineMath tex={"k=0"} /> is the clean sample and <InlineMath tex={"k=K"} /> is pure noise.**
  **<InlineMath tex={"\\lambda \\in [0,1]"} />** is continuous flow time, with the flow-matching literature's orientation kept: **<InlineMath tex={"\\lambda = 0"} /> is noise and <InlineMath tex={"\\lambda = 1"} /> is data.**
</Aside>

The choice of <InlineMath tex={"k"} /> was decided by the one expression in which both indices appear, the action chunk <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{a}^k_{t:t+H}"} /> — environment time as a subscript range, denoising step as a superscript.

<Aside>
  **Direction warning.** <InlineMath tex={"k"} /> counts **up into noise**; <InlineMath tex={"\\lambda"} /> counts **up into data**. They run opposite ways. Both orientations are kept because both are what their literatures use and re-orienting either would break every equation a reader might look up. The bridge is <InlineMath tex={"\\lambda \\leftrightarrow 1 - k/K"} />.
</Aside>

## Deliberate reuse

Five symbols carry more than one meaning. Each is disambiguated by context and by a note in the text at first use.

**<InlineMath tex={"\\lambda"} />** is normalized progress along a path (Chapter 2) and continuous flow time (Chapter 6). They never appear in the same chapter and mean structurally the same thing: fractional progress from one end of something to the other. Each chapter states which end is which.

**<InlineMath tex={"Q"} />, <InlineMath tex={"K"} />, <InlineMath tex={"V"} />** are attention matrices in Chapter 7 and value functions elsewhere. Three cues separate them: attention's appear as unsubscripted capitals inside matrix products such as <InlineMath tex={"QK^\\top"} />; the value functions always carry arguments or a parameter subscript, as in <InlineMath tex={"Q_\\phi(s,a)"} /> or <InlineMath tex={"V^\\pi(s)"} />; and they live in different chapters. Renaming attention's was rejected because every paper and every reader uses them.

**<InlineMath tex={"\\beta"} />** is the diffusion noise schedule, always subscripted as <InlineMath tex={"\\beta_k"} />, and the KL penalty weight in the GRPO objective, always bare and multiplying a divergence.

**<InlineMath tex={"\\epsilon"} />** is standard-normal noise almost everywhere. Two chapter-local exceptions: Chapter 3's per-step error probability in the <InlineMath tex={"O(\\epsilon T^2)"} /> bound, kept because the imitation-learning literature is unanimous and no Gaussian noise appears in that chapter; and "<InlineMath tex={"\\epsilon"} />-greedy" in Chapter 4, which is the name of an algorithm. The PPO clip range and TRPO bound were given subscripts specifically so they would not join this list.

**<InlineMath tex={"c"} />** is a conditioning input. Chapter 2's Kutzbach formula uses <InlineMath tex={"c_i"} /> for the constraints a joint imposes and Chapter 5's full PPO objective uses <InlineMath tex={"c_1, c_2"} /> as loss coefficients; both are subscripted and chapter-local. Chapter 3's zero-one imitation cost is written <InlineMath tex={"\\ell(s,a)"} /> to stay clear of all three.

<InlineMath tex={"\\mu"} /> and <InlineMath tex={"\\sigma"} /> always mean a mean and a standard deviation, of whatever object the subscript indicates, and need no disambiguation.

Finally: **<InlineMath tex={"\\pi_0"} /> and <InlineMath tex={"\\pi_{0.5}"} /> are the names of models**, not policies indexed by a parameter value. They appear upright and never inside a policy expression.

## Typographic conventions

Vectors and matrices are bold where the distinction carries weight — <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{T}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{J}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{a}_{t:t+H}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{r}"} /> — and states and actions are left light, because the lectures never bold them and doing so book-wide would add noise to hundreds of expressions. Sets and spaces are calligraphic, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathcal{S}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathcal{A}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathcal{D}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathcal{B}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathcal{K}"} />; number sets are blackboard, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbb{R}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbb{T}^2"} />. Operators are upright: <InlineMath tex={"\\mathrm{softmax}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathrm{clip}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathrm{sg}"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\arg\\max"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbb{E}"} />.

Estimates carry hats — <InlineMath tex={"\\hat a"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\hat A_i"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\hat x_0"} /> — and frozen or slowly-moving copies carry overbars — <InlineMath tex={"\\bar\\phi"} />, <InlineMath tex={"\\bar\\alpha_k"} />. Never both. Where an environment index and a process index meet, time is the subscript and the process is the superscript, as in <InlineMath tex={"\\mathbf{a}^k_{t:t+H}"} />.

Numbers read off a chart rather than printed on it are always prefixed with <InlineMath tex={"\\approx"} /> and identified as such, so that no figure in this book implies more precision than its source has.
